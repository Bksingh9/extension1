## PART 1: WHAT IS AGENT SIMULATION? (0:40 - 2:30)

"Traditional stock analysis looks at charts, earnings, and ratios. That's backward-looking data."

"Agent simulation is different. Instead of asking 'what happened?', you ask 'how would real people react?'"

"Here's the idea: you create AI agents with specific personalities. A conservative retiree investing for income. An aggressive day trader chasing momentum. A hedge fund analyst looking for mispricing. A CNBC anchor shaping the narrative."

"Then you give them all the same scenario and let them interact. Buy, sell, react to each other, shift the narrative. Over 30 simulated trading days."

"The tool that makes this possible is called MiroFish. It's an open-source AI agent simulation framework."

## PART 3: RESULTS BREAKDOWN (4:30 - 7:30)

"Here's what happened over 30 simulated trading days."

[Screen: Simulation data visualized]

"Day 1-3: Initial euphoria. Retail FOMO buyers pile in. Stock jumps 12% in the simulation."

"Day 4-7: First pullback. Day traders take profits. Media narrative shifts to 'is it overvalued?' The stock drops 5%."

"Day 8-15: This is where it gets interesting. Institutional agents start accumulating. Quietly. While retail is panicking about the pullback, the smart money is buying."

"Day 16-25: Analyst upgrades trickle in. Price targets raised. Retail comes back. Stock recovers and pushes to new highs."

"Day 26-30: Consolidation. Volume drops. Everyone's waiting for the next catalyst."

"The key pattern: initial retail euphoria → quick pullback → institutional accumulation → sustained recovery."

---

## PART 4: DID IT MATCH REALITY? (7:30 - 9:00)

"I ran this simulation before NVIDIA's actual earnings report last quarter."

"The actual pattern? Almost identical. Initial spike, pullback in the first week, institutional buying on the dip, recovery over the next month."

"The timing was off by a few days. The magnitude was different. But the behavioral pattern -- the sequence of events -- was remarkably close."

"That's the real value. Not predicting the exact price, but understanding the behavioral dynamics."

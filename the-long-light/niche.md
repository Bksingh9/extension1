# The Long Light — Niche strategy

## Audience
Curious adults (28–48, global English) who watch Johnny Harris, ColdFusion, Storied, and Real Stories — but want documentary patience without on-camera presenters or sponsorship reads.

## Promise
One true story per episode, told slowly, built around place, time, and the people who were in the room. Every fact sourced. New episode every 10 days.

## Voice & tone
**Patient. Cinematic. Empathetic.** See `the-long-light/brand.md` for the sample read and voice rules.

## Editorial guardrails (the moat)
- **Single anchor rule.** Every episode anchors on one person, one place, or one object. No anonymous "the establishment" narration.
- **Every fact sourced.** Sources go in `episodes/NNN/research.md` and the description. Same standard as The Dossier — different visual language.
- **No verdicts.** We tell stories. We do not editorialize. The viewer reaches their own conclusion.
- **Empathy rule.** Victims are named when they are named in the public record. Suspects never charged are not named.
- **Out of scope.** Speculative conspiracy without sourcing · ongoing court cases that could materially affect lives · ethnic / religious framing of geopolitical events · anything we can't ground in primary material.

## Top 10 candidate topics (ranked by fit + demand-to-supply gap)

1. **The Last Pilot at Borispol** — *Hook:* On December 25th, 1991, at 7:32 p.m. Moscow time, the Soviet Union ended. A Tu-134 crew sat on a runway in Kyiv, engines warm, waiting for orders that never came. They were the last crew in the air for a country that no longer existed.

2. **The Submarine That Refused to Sink (K-19)** — *Hook:* In July 1961, the Soviet nuclear submarine K-19 lost coolant to its reactor. Twenty-two men walked into the engine room knowing what would happen. The story was classified for thirty years.

3. **The Stasi Library** — *Hook:* East Germany kept files on a third of its population. When the wall fell, the citizens were allowed to read their own. Today the archive is open. We read three of them, with permission.

4. **The Light at Vela** — *Hook:* On September 22nd, 1979, an American satellite recorded a double flash of light near the South Indian Ocean. South Africa? Israel? Both? Forty-five years later, no government has ever confirmed what it saw.

5. **The Defector Who Came Back (Yurchenko)** — *Hook:* In August 1985, a senior KGB officer named Vitaly Yurchenko walked into the American embassy in Rome. Ninety-three days later, he walked out of a Washington restaurant — and went home. This is what happened in between.

6. **The Houseboat on Lake Geneva** — *Hook:* For nine months in 1942, the British codebreaker who'd helped break Enigma lived on a small boat moored on Lake Geneva. We trace what was inside, and why.

7. **The Camera That Ended a War (Eddie Adams, Saigon, 1968)** — *Hook:* A single photograph from a single street, taken by a single American photographer on the second day of February, 1968, changed how a nation thought about a war. The photographer himself spent the next thirty years trying to take it back.

8. **The Weather Station in Labrador** — *Hook:* In October 1943, a German U-boat installed an automated weather station on the coast of Labrador. It was the only armed German presence on the North American mainland during the war. Nobody noticed. It was found in 1981.

9. **Vela Hotel — The Satellite That Saw Too Much** — *Hook:* It was built to watch for nuclear tests above ground. It saw thousands of false positives, two real ones, and one that nobody can still agree about.

10. **The Last Telegram** — *Hook:* On July 14th, 2013, Western Union sent its final telegram. We retrace the 161-year arc — from a single wire strung between Baltimore and Washington, to three sentences sent in 2013 to a man who didn't know they'd be the last.

## Cadence & format
- **Length:** 8–14 minutes for the first 12 episodes (longer than Dossier; shorter than Harris).
- **Cadence:** every 10 days — slower than weekly. Room for craft.
- **Pilot target:** ship within 21 days.

## What "winning" looks like at 90 days
9 episodes shipped, 1k subscribers, one breakout (>10× average views) on a single-character story (most likely Borispol or Yurchenko — single-person arcs travel furthest).

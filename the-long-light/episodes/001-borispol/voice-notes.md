# Episode 001 — Voice-over delivery notes

> Read this once before recording. Print it next to the script.

## General delivery rules

- **Tempo.** Slower than ordinary documentary narration. Aim for **180–195 wpm**, dropping to **150 wpm** in emotional beats. Reference: Attenborough at his calmest, not Harris at his fastest. The script is timed at 200 wpm conversational, but the actual VO will run a touch slower with the pauses below.
- **Breath.** Take a full silent breath at every scene boundary (visible as `[SCENE N]` in the script). Take a half-breath at every paragraph break inside a scene. Breath = part of the sound design, not a flaw to edit out.
- **Pauses.** Two-beat pause (≈0.6s) after every ellipsis or em-dash. Three-beat pause (≈1.0s) before any date or number that matters. Five-beat pause (≈1.6s) after the end-card line.
- **Tonal palette.** Default tone: low, warm, slightly reflective. Avoid newsreader cadence. Avoid up-talk. Avoid sentence-end rises. End every sentence on a downward inflection.
- **Eye contact.** None — there is no presenter. But narrate as if speaking directly to one person across a table.
- **What never to do.** No emphasis on filler words (*"actually"*, *"really"*, *"basically"*). No vocal smiling. No exclamation. No theatrical whisper. No vocal fry at sentence ends.

## Per-scene cues

### Scene 1 — Cold Open (0:00–0:18)

> *"It is a quiet evening in Kyiv. The sun has already gone…"*

- **Tempo:** ~170 wpm. Slowest section of the episode.
- **Tone:** Conversational, almost intimate. Like the first line of a bedtime story for adults.
- **Stress on:** *"quiet"*, *"already gone"*, *"told to wait"*, *"already ended"*.
- **Pause:** 1.2s after *"told to wait."* Hold while the visual lands. 1.5s before *"This is The Long Light."* — that line is the brand handoff.
- **Mood note:** Lower the energy on every verb. The runway is supposed to feel small.

### Scene 2 — The Day Before (0:18–1:30)

> *"To understand what is happening in this cockpit, we need to go back four months…"*

- **Tempo:** ~190 wpm. Picks up to documentary cadence.
- **Tone:** Patient lecture-mode. You are explaining something carefully.
- **Stress on:** the dates — *"24th of August"*, *"1st of December"*, *"92.3%"*, *"8th of December"*. Each date gets a 0.5s pre-pause.
- **Pause:** 1.0s after *"in plain language"* before reading the quoted text. The quote in italics gets its own slower delivery — drop to 160 wpm for those two lines.
- **Mood note:** The Belavezha quote is the first cinematic hit. Read it like you are reading from the actual document.

### Scene 3 — The Speech (1:30–2:55)

> *"It is seven in the evening, Moscow time…"*

- **Tempo:** Drops back to ~175 wpm. This scene has weight.
- **Tone:** Slightly mournful, but not dramatic. Witness, not eulogist.
- **Stress on:** *"twelve minutes"*, *"chooses not to use force"*, *"He has stopped being President"*, *"thirty-two minutes past seven"*.
- **Pause:** 0.8s after *"He says he has stopped being President."* The line lands. Don't push.
- **Quoted text:** *"…I am ceasing my activity in the post of President of the USSR."* — drop to 150 wpm. Add 0.4s pause after *"USSR."* before continuing.
- **Mood note:** The flag-lowering paragraph is the emotional spike of the scene. Slow to 160 wpm for *"It happens quickly. There is no ceremony."* Each sentence its own beat.

### Scene 4 — Borispol (2:55–4:30)

> *"Eight hundred kilometers southwest, in Kyiv…"*

- **Tempo:** ~185 wpm. Steady.
- **Tone:** Returns to the cold-open intimacy.
- **Composite-framing paragraph:** Slow to 160 wpm for *"This is where the story we are telling tonight becomes a story about waiting…"* through *"any one of dozens of Tu-134 captains across the country could have been the man we are imagining."* — this is the transparency moment. Give it weight.
- **Stress on:** *"twenty years"*, *"deeply familiar"*, *"It is now nearly eight"*, *"He does not yet understand what he has just heard."*
- **Pause:** 1.2s after *"He does not yet understand what he has just heard."* Heaviest pause in the script.

### Scene 5 — The Question (4:30–6:00)

> *"The flight plan in his hand is a Soviet flight plan…"*

- **Tempo:** ~190 wpm. The scene is information-dense.
- **Tone:** Slightly more analytical. You are mapping the chaos.
- **Stress on:** *"USSR"* in the first sentence, *"a piece of paper from a country that no longer exists"*, *"In Russian"*, *"In Ukrainian"*, *"In both"*.
- **Pause:** 0.6s after each of *"In Russian. ... In Ukrainian. ... In both."* The three short sentences are the rhythm of the scene.
- **Mood note:** The closing line — *"There is no way to know, in the moment, who the last one truly was."* — drop to 150 wpm. Land it like the closing of a paragraph in a book.

### Scene 6 — The Long Light (6:00–7:30)

> *"This is what most of us know about the end of the Soviet Union…"*

- **Tempo:** ~165 wpm. Slowest scene after cold open.
- **Tone:** Reflective. This is the thesis of the channel, in miniature.
- **Stress on:** *"the speech"*, *"the flag"*, *"a fisherman in Vladivostok"*, *"a teacher in Tashkent"*, *"almost nothing visible changed"*.
- **Pause:** 1.0s after each name in the montage list. Give each city its weight.
- **Mood note:** *"Forty minutes later, when they were over the Black Sea, they were no longer flying for the Soviet Union. They were flying for a country that had not yet decided what to call them."* — read this almost as one breath. Slight lift on *"that had not yet decided"*.

### Scene 7 — End Card (7:30–8:00)

> *"We don't know that captain's name…"*

- **Tempo:** ~150 wpm. Slowest part of the episode.
- **Tone:** Quiet. Almost private.
- **Stress on:** *"don't know"* (three times), *"about seventeen minutes longer"*.
- **Pause:** 1.5s before *"He waited about seventeen minutes longer than he expected to."* — the final image of the episode.
- **End line:** *"…and that's where we leave it. For now."* — this is the brand sign-off. Same delivery every episode. **150 wpm, downward inflection on every word, full stop at "now"**, then **2.0s of silence before fadeout**.
- **Mood note:** Don't smile. Don't soften. Just leave it.

## Recording technical notes

- **Mic:** condenser at ~15cm, slight off-axis to avoid plosives.
- **Room:** treated. The Long Light cannot have room reverb — kills the intimacy.
- **Take strategy:** record every scene three times. The third take is usually the one. Don't do whole-script takes — scene by scene.
- **Editing:** keep all breaths in. Remove only saliva clicks. Do NOT compress — the dynamic range *is* the show.
- **Music drop:** music enters at 0:18 (end of cold open), peaks at 5:30, drops to silence by 7:30. Voice carries the end card alone.

## After recording

Run through `pipeline/voiceover.py` (existing) for the rendered video, but **manually edit** the resulting `voiceover.wav` against the recorded VO before muxing. The pipeline's auto-pacing won't catch the 1.2s "He does not yet understand" pause; that has to be honored manually.

## Positioning memo (the angle gap)

The dominant YouTube treatments of Gladio are: (a) the 1992 BBC Timewatch documentary by Allan Francovich, ~2h25m, re-uploaded many times; (b) a 2009 History Channel documentary; (c) long, often un-sourced "exposé" channel uploads. **There is no calm, modern, citation-first 6-minute explainer that visually distinguishes verified court findings from still-disputed allegations.** That is The Dossier's lane. Red for what the documents and sentences say. Amber for what is alleged. Teal for what cross-checks against primary sources. The viewer leaves knowing exactly what is proven, what is contested, and where to read the evidence themselves.

## Sourced facts (verified web-stage)

> Color tag = visual signal in the script:
>   `[RED]` court-finding / officially declassified / damning fact

>   `[TEAL]` cross-checked against primary source

1. **`[RED]` On 24 October 1990, Italian PM Giulio Andreotti acknowledged before the Italian parliament the existence of a clandestine NATO-coordinated stay-behind network in Italy known as Gladio.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Gladio* (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gladio) — confirmed.
   - History and Headlines, *October 24, 1990* — confirmed.

2. **`[RED]` Andreotti's submission to the Commissione Stragi listed 622 civilians as members of Gladio and reported that 127 weapons caches had been dismantled.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Gladio* — quotes Andreotti's report verbatim. (NB: the figure for arms caches is sometimes cited as 139 in secondary literature; **127 is what Andreotti himself put on the record** — that is the one we use, with attribution.)

3. **`[RED]` On 22 November 1990 the European Parliament adopted a resolution on the Gladio affair, published in OJ C 324/201 (Official Journal of the European Communities).**
   - EUR-Lex, *OJ JOC 1990 324 R 0186 01*, published 24 Dec 1990.
   - Wikisource, *European Parliament resolution on Gladio* (full text mirror).
   - Publications Office of the EU — *RESOLUTION on the Gladio affair* (multiple language versions).

4. **`[RED]` The resolution explicitly named the existence "for 40 years" of clandestine parallel intelligence and armed-operations organisations in several Member States, "which escaped all democratic controls" and were "run by the secret services of the states concerned in collaboration with NATO".**
   - Quoted in Wikisource and OJ C 324/201. Verbatim language.

5. **`[RED]` The Belgian government publicly disclosed the Belgian section on 7 November 1990 (Defence Minister Guy Coëme + PM Wilfried Martens). The Council of Ministers ordered it disbanded on 23 November 1990.**
   - Wikipedia, *Belgian stay-behind network* — confirmed dates, names of network: **SDRA-VIII** (Service de Documentation, de Renseignements et d'Action VIII), under Belgian military intelligence (SGR).

6. **`[RED]` Stay-behind structures were confirmed in (at minimum) Belgium, Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Norway, plus Greece, Turkey, Switzerland, Austria, Portugal, Spain. The "Allied Clandestine Committee" (ACC) coordinated them. Sweden ("Sveaborg") and Finland were disclosed later.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Gladio* — country list and ACC reference.
   - Daniele Ganser, *NATO's Secret Armies* (Frank Cass, 2005) — cross-reference (book listing confirmed via Routledge / tandfonline).

7. **`[RED]` On 2 August 1980 a time-bomb exploded in the second-class waiting room of Bologna Centrale at 10:25 a.m., killing 85 people and wounding more than 200.**
   - Wikipedia, *Bologna massacre* — confirmed.
   - Britannica, *Bologna train station bombing of 1980* — confirmed.

8. **`[RED]` Convictions for the Bologna massacre, by Italian Supreme Court (Corte di Cassazione):**
   - Valerio Fioravanti, Francesca Mambro — life, upheld 23 Nov 1995.
   - Luigi Ciavardini — 30 years, upheld April 2007.
   - Gilberto Cavallini — life, upheld 27 Sep 2023 (per ANSA).
   - Paolo Bellini ("the fifth man") — life, April 2022 (per ANSA, Searchlight).
   - All neo-fascist militants (NAR / Avanguardia Nazionale).

9. **`[RED]` In January 2021 the Bologna Assize Court of Appeal ruled in writing that the bombing was "un strage di Stato" — "a State massacre" — and named as masterminders Licio Gelli (head of the P2 Masonic Lodge), Umberto Ortolani, Federico Umberto D'Amato, and Mario Tedeschi. The court explicitly stated D'Amato "had links with NATO, the Stay-Behind/Gladio organisation".**
   - ANSA (English), *Bologna bombing was 'State massacre' says court* (8 Jan 2021).
   - Atlas of Wars, *The Bologna massacre*.
   - This is the single most consequential post-1990 fact — it is a *court ruling*, not an allegation, that names the Gladio link.

10. **`[AMBER]` The "strategy of tension" (*strategia della tensione*) thesis — that elements connected to stay-behind networks colluded with far-right militants to discredit the Italian left through political violence in the 1970s — has support from former Italian magistrates and from minority reports of the Commissione Stragi, but is not a settled academic finding.**
    - Wikipedia, *Operation Gladio* — frames the thesis as widely cited but contested.
    - Searchlight Magazine, *The 'stay behind' NATO operation behind the Bologna bombing* (Aug 2025) — proponent view.
    - The Tandfonline review of Daniele Ganser's *NATO's Secret Armies* — critical / sceptical view ("Falling Flat on the Stay-Behinds", 2006).

11. **`[AMBER]` The "Brabant killers" carried out a series of armed robberies/attacks in the Belgian province of Brabant from 1982 to 1985, killing 28 people and wounding 22. A Gladio link has been suggested by some investigators; an official Belgian parliamentary inquiry "found no substantive evidence that the network was involved in any terrorist acts".**
    - Wikipedia, *Brabant killers* — death toll and inquiry conclusion.
    - Wikipedia, *Belgian stay-behind network* — link to inquiry.

12. **`[RED]` Former CIA Director William Colby acknowledged in his 1978 memoir *Honorable Men: My Life in the CIA* (Simon & Schuster) his role in setting up stay-behind networks; he served at the U.S. embassy in Sweden (1951–53) and Italy (1953–58).** *(Page citation pending — Chapter 3 "A Scandinavian Spy" covers Sweden, pp. 78–107; Italy work covered in subsequent chapters.)*
    - Wikipedia, *William Colby* — service dates and memoir confirmed.
    - Spyscape, *Spies and Saboteurs: The CIA Plot to Defend Europe* — quotes Colby on stay-behind setup.
    - Scribd mirror of Chapter 3 used to confirm Sweden chapter pagination.

## What this episode will not claim

- Direct CIA orchestration of any specific massacre. The 2021 Bologna ruling names individuals (Gelli, D'Amato, etc.) and links them to the Stay-Behind/Gladio structure; we report the court's exact language and stop there.
- Any name not in court records or official inquiry reports.
- Any number contradicted by Andreotti's own report — we side with what the Italian PM told the parliament, with attribution.

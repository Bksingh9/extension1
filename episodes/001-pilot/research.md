## Positioning memo (the angle gap)

The top YouTube treatments of Gladio fall into two camps: short "weird history" recaps that treat the story as trivia, and long-form conspiracy framings that conflate verified fact with allegation and lose the audience's trust. **The Dossier's lane is the one neither camp owns: a calm, document-by-document walkthrough that makes the verified record visually unmistakable from the still-disputed claims.** Red for what the documents say. Amber for what is alleged. Teal for what cross-checks against primary sources. The audience leaves knowing exactly what is proven, what is contested, and where to read it themselves. That trust is the channel.

## Sourced facts (12, seed — verify each)

> Format: **fact** — *source* `[VERIFY]`

1. **On 24 October 1990, Italian Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti disclosed to parliament the existence of a clandestine NATO-coordinated "stay-behind" network in Italy known as Gladio.** — Italian Chamber of Deputies record, 24 Oct 1990; Andreotti's written report to the Parliamentary Commission on Terrorism. `[VERIFY: locate Camera dei Deputati transcript]`

2. **On 22 November 1990 the European Parliament passed a resolution condemning the existence of stay-behind networks and calling for full investigation.** — European Parliament Resolution on the Gladio affair, OJ C 324, 24 Dec 1990. `[VERIFY: europarl.europa.eu archive]`

3. **The original NATO doctrine, "stay-behind", was conceived in the late 1940s to organise resistance cells in the event of a Soviet occupation of Western Europe.** — Daniele Ganser, *NATO's Secret Armies* (Frank Cass, 2005); declassified CIA history "Origins of the CIA". `[VERIFY: cross-check Ganser ch. 2 + CIA FOIA reading room]`

4. **Stay-behind networks were confirmed in at least: Italy, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Denmark, Norway, West Germany, Greece, Turkey, France, Switzerland, Austria, Portugal, Spain — and later disclosures added Sweden ("Sveaborg") and Finland.** — EU Parliament 1990 debate transcripts; subsequent national parliamentary inquiries. `[VERIFY: each country's inquiry separately]`

5. **The Italian Gladio network had 622 confirmed members and 139 buried arms caches at the time of disclosure.** — Italian Parliamentary Commission on Terrorism final report (Commissione Stragi). `[VERIFY: senato.it archive]`

6. **The Belgian Senate opened an inquiry in November 1990; its 1991 report confirmed the existence of the Belgian SDRA-VIII network and noted unresolved questions about possible links to the 1982–1985 "Brabant massacres" that killed 28 people.** — Belgian Senate inquiry report, 1991. `[VERIFY: senate.be archive]`

7. **The 1980 Bologna train station bombing killed 85 people and injured over 200; Italian courts ultimately convicted neo-fascist militants Valerio Fioravanti and Francesca Mambro. The relationship of the bombing to Gladio remains formally disputed but was raised by the Commissione Stragi.** — Italian court records, multiple appellate rulings 1988–2020; Commissione Stragi reports. `[VERIFY: Cassazione rulings + Commission citations]`

8. **The "strategy of tension" (*strategia della tensione*) thesis — that elements connected to stay-behind networks colluded with far-right actors to discredit the Italian left through political violence in the 1970s — has support from former Italian magistrates and parliamentary inquiries but is not a settled finding.** — Felice Casson testimony; General Gerardo Serravalle testimony; Commissione Stragi minority reports. `[VERIFY: each cited testimony]`

9. **The P2 Masonic Lodge (Propaganda Due), led by Licio Gelli, was found by a 1981 Italian parliamentary commission to have been a covert organisation involved in subversion of the state; its membership list overlapped with Italian intelligence services. The full Gladio overlap is contested.** — Anselmi Commission Report, 1984. `[VERIFY: parliamentary archive]`

10. **Former CIA Director William Colby acknowledged U.S. funding of Italian stay-behind structures in his 1978 memoir *Honorable Men*.** — William Colby, *Honorable Men: My Life in the CIA* (Simon & Schuster, 1978), pp. 128–139. `[VERIFY: page numbers]`

11. **In 2000 the Italian Senate's Commissione Stragi released a final report stating, in its majority, that some massacres of the 1969–1980 period had been carried out "with the collaboration of the structures of information of the United States."** — Commissione Parlamentare di Inchiesta sul Terrorismo in Italia, *Relazione finale*, 2000 (majority report by Sen. Pellegrino). `[VERIFY: senato.it]`

12. **Sweden disclosed in 2014 that its post-war stay-behind organisation, "Sveaborg", was active until at least 1990; Norway, Denmark, and Switzerland published similar disclosures in the 1990s and 2000s.** — Swedish Defence Ministry release, 2014; Norwegian *Lund Commission*, 1996; Swiss *PUK EMD* report, 1990. `[VERIFY: each disclosure separately]`

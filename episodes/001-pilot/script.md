[SCENE 1 — 0:00–0:08]
On the 24th of October 1990, the Prime Minister of Italy stood up in parliament and admitted his government had been running a secret army for forty-five years. Its name was Gladio.

[SCENE 2 — 0:08–0:25]
The story does not start in Italy. It starts in 1947, with NATO and a doctrine called "stay-behind". The idea was simple: if the Soviets ever rolled west, leave armed cells inside every NATO country to fight the occupation from underground. Cells with weapons. Cells with codenames. Cells with orders.

[SCENE 3 — 0:25–0:45]
In Italy alone, when the network was finally disclosed, prosecutors counted six hundred and twenty-two members, and a hundred and thirty-nine buried arms caches. Belgium had one. So did the Netherlands. So did Norway, Denmark, Greece, Turkey, France, Switzerland, Austria, Portugal, Spain, West Germany — and, later disclosures showed, Sweden and Finland.

[SCENE 4 — 0:45–1:00]
Officially, they were there to fight Soviets who never came. What they actually did, between 1947 and 1990, is the rest of this dossier. We are going to walk through it the way the documents do — what's proven, what's alleged, and what is still classified.

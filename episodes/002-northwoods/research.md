## Positioning memo (the angle gap)

Northwoods is far better-known than Gladio thanks to social-media virality (and frequent, often-distorted invocation by people like RFK Jr.). The dominant YouTube treatments are either (a) sensationalist "false flag" channels that conflate the rejected proposal with later events it had nothing to do with, or (b) calm-but-credentialed long-form documentaries that bury the actual document text. **The Dossier's wedge is to walk through the actual 13-page memorandum on screen, paragraph by paragraph, distinguishing what the Joint Chiefs *proposed* (red — the document says it) from what *happened* (nothing — JFK rejected) from what is sometimes *claimed* (amber — speculative connections to later events).** The teal goes on the document's own paragraph numbers. This is the citation-first treatment the topic has never had on YouTube.

## Sourced facts (12, verified web-stage)

> Tag = visual signal:
>   `[RED]` court-finding / declassified / damning, sourced
>   `[AMBER]` allegation, contested
>   `[TEAL]` cross-checked against primary source

1. **`[RED]` On 13 March 1962, U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman General Lyman Lemnitzer transmitted a top-secret memorandum titled "Justification for U.S. Military Intervention in Cuba" to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods*.
   - National Security Archive (NSArchive), GWU — primary host of the declassified PDF: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20010430/northwoods.pdf

2. **`[TEAL]` The document was an enclosure to a longer JCS memorandum and ran approximately 13 pages of proposed pretext operations.**
   - Mary Ferrell Foundation, *Operation Northwoods*.
   - NSArchive — full PDF.

3. **`[RED]` The memorandum was written in response to a request from Brig. Gen. Edward Lansdale, chief of operations for the anti-Castro Cuba Project ("Operation Mongoose"), for "pretexts which would provide justification for U.S. military intervention in Cuba".**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Mongoose*.
   - NSArchive briefing book, *Kennedy and Cuba: Operation Mongoose*.

4. **`[RED]` Pretext options proposed in the document included: (a) staging the explosion of a U.S. Navy ship in Guantánamo Bay; (b) "remote-control" of a civilian aircraft secretly painted as a U.S. Air Force plane and faking its destruction; (c) sinking boats of Cuban refugees on the high seas; (d) orchestrating "terror" in Miami, Washington, and other U.S. cities; (e) fabricating a "shoot-down" of a U.S. military aircraft off the coast of Cuba.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods* — verbatim list of proposals.
   - NSArchive press release, 30 April 2001.

5. **`[RED]` The document was prefaced by a 2 February 1962 internal memorandum, "Possible Actions to Provoke, Harass or Disrupt Cuba", drafted by Brig. Gen. William H. Craig and addressed to Lansdale, which fed material into the Northwoods proposals.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods*.

6. **`[RED]` The Joint Chiefs of Staff approved the Northwoods proposals before transmittal to the SecDef. President John F. Kennedy rejected the plan; none of its operations were implemented.**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods*.
   - State of Surveillance, *Operation Northwoods: The False Flag That Wasn't*.

7. **`[RED]` Three months after rejecting Northwoods, in late 1962, JFK reassigned Lemnitzer; Lemnitzer was not reappointed to the JCS chairmanship and was instead made Supreme Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR) at NATO.**
   - Wikipedia, *Lyman Lemnitzer*.

8. **`[RED]` The Northwoods document was declassified through the JFK Assassination Records Collection Act of 1992 and released in 1997 by the JFK Assassination Records Review Board (ARRB).**
   - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods*.
   - Britannica, *Operation Northwoods*.

9. **`[RED]` The National Security Archive at George Washington University obtained the document under FOIA and published it in full, with its cover memoranda, on 30 April 2001 — the form most widely circulated today.**
   - NSArchive press release, *Pentagon Proposed Pretexts for Cuba Invasion in 1962*: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20010430/

10. **`[RED]` The 1997 ARRB release covered approximately 1,521 pages of previously secret JCS records spanning 1962–1964; the Northwoods enclosure is one document within that release.**
    - Wikipedia, *Operation Northwoods*.

11. **`[AMBER]` The quote attributed to McNamara — that the military was "proposing things that were completely insane" — appears in his later reflections, but in a separate interview McNamara stated he had "absolutely zero recollection" of the specific Northwoods document. We will quote both, with attribution, and let the audience hold the contradiction.**
    - State of Surveillance, *The False Flag That Wasn't*.
    - Multiple secondary sources cite both McNamara comments.

12. **`[AMBER]` Northwoods is frequently invoked online to support claims about later events (most prominently 9/11). Those claims are not supported by the Northwoods document, which was rejected and never implemented. We will explicitly flag this on screen — the document proves what the Joint Chiefs *proposed* in 1962, not what happened in any subsequent decade.**
    - The Conversation, *What RFK Jr. Didn't Tell You About 'Operation Northwoods'*.

## What this episode will not claim

- Any link between Northwoods and any post-1962 event. The document is what the Joint Chiefs proposed and JFK rejected. That is the story.
- Any McNamara quote without attribution to the specific source and an acknowledgement of his contradicting statement.
- Any speculation about Lemnitzer's reassignment as "punishment" — we report the dates and let the viewer connect them.

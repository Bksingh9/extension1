[SCENE 1 — Hook — 0:00–0:08]
On the 13th of March, 1962, the Chairman of the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff put a thirteen-page document on Robert McNamara's desk. It was a plan to fake terrorist attacks on American cities, and blame them on Cuba.

[SCENE 2 — Mongoose — 0:08–0:30]  *(scaffold)*
- Operation Mongoose. The Cuba Project. Edward Lansdale.
- Lansdale's request to the Joint Chiefs: "pretexts which would provide justification for U.S. military intervention in Cuba".
- Why the Pentagon answered. Cold War context, Bay of Pigs aftermath.

[SCENE 3 — Memo — 0:30–1:10]  *(scaffold)*
- Document on screen: "Justification for U.S. Military Intervention in Cuba (TS)".
- Date: 13 March 1962. Signed: Gen. Lyman Lemnitzer, JCS Chairman.
- Recipient: SecDef Robert McNamara. Classified TOP SECRET.

[SCENE 4 — Pretexts — 1:10–2:30]  *(scaffold)*
- Pretext 1: blow up a U.S. Navy ship in Guantánamo Bay.
- Pretext 2: civilian aircraft repainted as USAF, faked destruction.
- Pretext 3: sink boats of Cuban refugees on the high seas.
- Pretext 4: orchestrate terror in Miami, Washington, and other U.S. cities.
- Pretext 5: fake the shoot-down of a U.S. military aircraft.
- Each red on screen, with the line number from the actual document.

[SCENE 5 — NoEvent — 2:30–3:00]  *(scaffold)*
- The Joint Chiefs approved it. McNamara forwarded it.
- President Kennedy rejected it within days.
- Nothing in the document was implemented.

[SCENE 6 — Lemnitzer — 3:00–3:45]  *(scaffold)*
- Three months after the rejection, Lemnitzer was not reappointed as JCS Chairman.
- Reassigned to NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR).
- Dates only. No editorialising.

[SCENE 7 — Buried — 3:45–4:30]  *(scaffold)*
- The document stayed classified for 35 years.
- 1992: JFK Assassination Records Collection Act passed.
- 1997: Assassination Records Review Board declassifies the JCS records (~1,521 pages).
- 30 April 2001: National Security Archive publishes the document in full.

[SCENE 8 — McNamara — 4:30–5:00]  *(scaffold)*
- McNamara, looking back: "We had military officers proposing things that were completely insane."
- McNamara, asked directly about Northwoods: "I have absolutely zero recollection of it. But I sure as hell would have rejected it."
- Both attributed. Both on screen at once. Let the audience hold the contradiction.

[SCENE 9 — Misuse — 5:00–5:40]  *(scaffold)*
- What Northwoods proves: what the Joint Chiefs proposed, on paper, in 1962.
- What Northwoods does not prove: anything about any later event.
- Amber column: "frequently invoked to support claims about events Northwoods has nothing to do with."

[SCENE 10 — Filed — 5:40–6:00]  *(scaffold)*
- "What we still don't know — what other proposals were drafted and never released — is in the rest of the 1,521 pages."
- "Next dossier: MKUltra. The 20,000 pages everyone has heard of. We read them."
- "Filed."

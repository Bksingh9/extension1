- 2,000 retail investors (varying risk tolerance, emotional trading patterns)

**Seed data**: I fed it real financial news articles from the past 6 months about tech earnings, layoff announcements, and market reactions.

**Parameters**:
- 30 simulation rounds (representing 30 trading days)
- Each agent has memory (remembers previous rounds)
- Agents can influence each other (media affects retail sentiment, etc.)

### THE RESULTS (5:00 - 9:00)

**[SCREEN -- results dashboard with charts]**

"Alright. Here's what 5,000 AI agents did with a mixed earnings signal. And this is where it gets really interesting.

**Day 1-3: The Split**

The market immediately divided. Institutional investors focused on the earnings beat -- they saw value. Retail investors focused on the layoffs -- they saw risk. The stock went UP 3% on day 1 because institutional money moved first.

**Day 4-7: The Media Effect**

Here's where it gets wild. The financial media agents started running with the layoff story because negative headlines get more clicks. Look at this sentiment graph -- media coverage went 70% negative even though the earnings were positive.

This flipped retail sentiment hard. Social media agents started posting about 'the next big tech crash.' Classic amplification loop.

**Day 8-15: The Correction**

The stock dropped 8% from its day-1 high. Not because of fundamentals -- the earnings were great -- but because of narrative. The simulation showed that media-driven sentiment can temporarily overpower fundamental analysis.

And here's the fascinating part -- the AI day traders made money on the way up AND the way down. They were the only group that consistently profited, because they traded on momentum regardless of narrative.

**Day 16-25: The Recovery**

As the layoff news cycle faded, institutional investors started accumulating again. Analyst agents published 'buy' ratings. The stock slowly recovered.

**Day 26-30: The New Equilibrium**

The stock ended up 5% above where it started -- roughly where the earnings beat alone would suggest. But the PATH to get there was chaotic.

**[SHOW CHART]**

Look at this price chart. If you just saw the start and end points, you'd think nothing happened. But the 30-day journey? Massive volatility, driven almost entirely by narrative, not fundamentals."

### WHAT THE AI GOT RIGHT AND WRONG (9:00 - 11:00)

**[ON CAMERA with comparison graphics]**

"Now here's where I did something interesting. I ran this simulation based on a scenario that ALREADY HAPPENED in real life -- I just didn't tell the AI that. I used a real tech company's earnings from 3 months ago.

**What the simulation got RIGHT:**
- The initial pop on earnings ✓
- The media pivot to negative narrative ✓
- The mid-month dip driven by sentiment ✓
- The eventual recovery to fundamentals ✓
- Day traders profiting from volatility ✓

**What it got WRONG:**
- The magnitude of the dip (simulation: -8%, reality: -5%)
- The timing of recovery (simulation: day 16, reality: day 12)
- It didn't predict the options activity that amplified the real move

**My take:** The simulation captured the SHAPE of what happened remarkably well. It understood the dynamics between different market participants. The exact numbers were off, but the behavioral patterns were spot-on.

And that's the real value here. You don't use this to pick stocks. You use it to understand HOW markets process information. That's the insight that's actually valuable."
